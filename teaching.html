Teaching Philosophy

My teaching philosophy centers on the integration of creative technology, design thinking, and hands-on experimentation. I believe students learn best when they are actively engaged in creating meaningful projects they are personally invested in. I teach students at all levels, from freshmen to graduate students, within the Visualization Program, and each course is intentionally scaffolded to develop well-rounded designers and problem solvers.

AI is a technology that educators have increasingly had to contend with over the past few years. Across my courses, I strive to strike a balance between introducing students to these tools while still encouraging them to rely on their own problem-solving abilities and design intuition. One of the primary risks of AI and large language models is their potential to diminish critical thinking, particularly for novice learners. Learning is inherently difficult, and when students rely too heavily on AI assistance, they miss opportunities to develop strong creative and technical foundations.

To address this challenge, I emphasize beginning the design process with hands-on activities such as sketching, journaling, and group brainstorming. Once students have articulated their own ideas and developed an understanding of user needs, they are then encouraged to consult LLMs to help expand concepts, locate references, or plan implementation strategies. Many students in the arts are initially resistant to AI—especially image generation—so I place particular emphasis on demonstrating ethical and thoughtful uses of the technology. For example, one student created a series of 2D illustrations for a character intended for a 3D model. I suggested using recent video generation models to produce a 3D turntable based on their illustrations. This approach preserved the student’s unique style while accelerating the early stages of the modeling process. Ultimately, my goal—for both myself and my students—is to create more time for iteration and refinement. When used critically, these tools can be both helpful and exciting for designers and artists. The key is maintaining strong critical thinking and problem-solving skills so students can effectively evaluate when and how AI is useful. Below, I outline how this philosophy is reflected across my courses.

In my freshman courses (VIST 110), I focus on instilling a foundational design thinking mindset that prepares students to take on a wide range of challenges. Students may later specialize in Animation, Game Development, or Interactive Design, so my responsibility is to equip them with a strong understanding of iterative design processes and human-centered workflows. In Design Innovation, we emphasize user research, hypothesis formation, prototyping, and documentation—skills that translate across disciplines and support future coursework. Students use tools such as Figma and the Adobe Suite to produce mockups and interactive prototypes that allow them to test and validate their ideas.

In my sophomore courses (VIST 210), the emphasis shifts toward technical skill development and the fundamentals of time-based and interactive storytelling. Unreal Engine plays a central role in this course due to its broad applicability across game development, animation, virtual production, and interactive design. The course includes two major individual projects: a one-minute animated music video and an interactive narrative in the form of a “choose your own adventure” experience. The music video project introduces students to linear, time-based media through camera work, lighting, rendering, animation, materials, and 3D editing. The interactive narrative project builds on this foundation by introducing blueprints, user interface design, and the challenges of user interaction. Because both projects are completed individually, students are able to build confidence in their technical and artistic skills before transitioning to larger team-based work.

With my junior and senior students, the focus shifts toward collaboration, team dynamics, and the production of polished, portfolio-ready projects. Within the Visualization Program, students participate in Vertical Studios, where sophomores, juniors, and seniors collaborate on semester-long projects. These courses are structured to mirror real-world production environments, requiring students to develop concepts, design for specific audiences, and deliver viable outcomes within fixed timelines. Students gain experience navigating team dynamics, managing project scope, and iterating on design decisions in response to feedback. Throughout this process, they are encouraged to explore emerging technologies while maintaining a strong grounding in design fundamentals.

Through this project-based approach, students develop not only technical proficiency but also critical thinking, problem-solving skills, and the confidence needed to address complex, real-world challenges in interactive design and digital media.

Courses

Below are the courses I currently teach, along with links to download syllabi where available.

VIST 110

Design Innovation

Introduction to fundamental design issues, processes, and theories relevant to design resolution and the creation of new ideas for innovative design products and services by keeping human needs at the center of the development process; Overview of design research, prototyping, evaluation, and communication techniques to generate valuable insights for design innovation.

VIST-110 Syllabus

Key Topics

VIST 210

Time and Interaction

Students will learn the language of time-based and interactive storytelling, with a focus on the skills needed to build a strong narrative experience and highly effective visuals for use in linear and non-linear motion graphics, and interactive projects. Students are expected to produce their own creative artifacts that grapple with the techniques and concepts related to time and interaction.

VIST-210 Syllabus

Key Topics

Principles of Animation Linear Narratives Interactive Narratives Unreal Engine

VIST 205, 305, 405

Interactive Vertical Studio

Allows students to explore experiential and interactive design through the creation of a semester long project of their own design. We will focus equally on the design process as well as producing and deploying our designs to be engaged with. Projects can take the form of art installations, exhibitions, themed attractions, interior design, event design, activations, etc. We will emphasize the pre-visualization of spaces and use 3-D technology to make design choices prior to deployment. In this studio students will focus their energy on creating a substantive project that combines visual thinking, design research, multimodality, interactivity, and storytelling.

Studio Syllabus

Key Topics

Team Project Based Interactive Design Creative Technology Branding Experience Design

VIST 289

Special Topics in TouchDesigner

Introduction to the fundamentals and practices of real-time interactive multimedia production through the use of the industry leading software TouchDesigner. Creation of interactive graphics, procedural visuals, data-driven systems and control systems through the use of node based programming. Topics include projection mapping, using sensors, creating user interfaces, interoperability, optimization and rendering. The course will consist of individual assignments to learn fundamentals and techniques with a culminating project at the end of the course.

Interactive Virtual Environments

Languages and techniques useful for the creation of real time virtual environments; definition of formal scene description structures; modeling and transformation techniques; simulation techniques; behaviors and message passing; user interaction and animation; multiuser environments; creating virtual interfaces; scripting techniques.
